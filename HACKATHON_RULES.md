# Hackathon Rules — Judging

Source: hackathon website, Judging page.

## Judging Panel

A mixed panel of 9 judges, including:

- Accessibility experts
- Technology experts
- Business experts
- Inclusive design / disability community representatives

Judges are present in person on the final afternoon. Winners are determined
through standardized scoring followed by judge deliberation.

## Two Evaluation Tracks

All teams are eligible for the monetary prizes and community/mentor-awarded
titles regardless of track.

1. **Open Challenge on Accessibility** — define your own accessibility
   problem and solution (education, healthcare, work, entertainment, or
   everyday essentials), for any target group (hearing, speech, visual,
   mental, physical, or temporary impairments).
2. **Pick a Challenge** — choose one of 16 published challenges submitted by
   real individuals/communities and solve the specific "how might we"
   question in that challenge.

## Presentation Format (Sunday)

- 6 minutes — present solution and demo
- 3 minutes — Q&A with judges (each judge may ask up to 1 question)

## Judging Criteria

Five equally-weighted categories:

1. **Relevance & Impact**
   - Does it solve a meaningful real-world accessibility problem (open theme
     or specific challenge)?
   - How measurable is the impact on daily life, independence, or inclusion
     of the target users?

2. **Innovation & Future Readiness**
   - How original and future-oriented is the idea?
   - Does it leverage AI or other emerging technologies?
   - Is it aligned with long-term digital accessibility and inclusion goals?

3. **Functionality, Feasibility & Scalability**
   - Is there a working prototype (proof of concept)?
   - How realistic is the proposed implementation and its scalability
     potential?
   - Can it integrate into existing ecosystems (devices, public services,
     banking, etc.)?

4. **Ethics, Security & Inclusion**
   - Does it comply with digital ethics, transparency, and personal data
     protection principles?
   - Is it designed with and for the people it aims to serve, ensuring
     genuine accessibility rather than assumptions about their needs?
   - Did the team show collaboration, fair competition, and support toward
     other teams?

5. **Presentation & Communication**
   - Was the solution presented effectively?
   - Is the presentation clear, professional, and engaging for both
     technical and non-technical judges?
   - Does it tell a coherent story: Problem → Solution → Impact?
